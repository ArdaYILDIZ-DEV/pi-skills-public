# Turkish into casual internet English

Translate the intended post, reply, DM, or social message. Do not translate drafting instructions or adopt quoted context as the user's own view.

## Preserve the message

Track actor, time, negation, conditions, quantities, uncertainty, willingness, intensity, and attribution.

- `yapabilirim` is ability or possibility, not automatically a promise.
- `sanırım`, `galiba`, and `olabilir` keep meaningful uncertainty near the relevant claim.
- `istemiyorum`, `sevmiyorum`, and `nefret ediyorum` are different levels of feeling.
- `uğraşmak istemiyorum` does not mean `I don't care`.
- `bende oluyor` reports the speaker's experience, not a universal product defect.
- Turkish may omit gender. Use singular `they`, a role, or natural restructuring rather than guessing.
- Determine whether `-miş` marks reported information, inference, or realization; do not mechanically use `apparently`.

## Rebuild, do not polish upward

Use natural English order, collocations, contractions, and paragraphing. Keep the user's frustration, humor, restraint, firmness, or uncertainty. Do not add apologies, therapeutic reassurance, slang, or a debate invitation.

Particles such as `ya`, `işte`, `hani`, `yani`, and `açıkçası` may mark emphasis, hesitation, explanation, or contrast. Preserve their function when it matters; do not translate each one as `well`, `you know`, `I mean`, `honestly`, or `tbh`.

Preserve exact technical terms, UI labels, prices, versions, units, and quotations. Do not invent context to make a sparse comment sound fuller.

## Two checks

1. Read only the English. Remove translation-shaped syntax and essay polish; keep useful conversational looseness.
2. Compare with the Turkish. Restore every fact, condition, stance, uncertainty, and attribution.

Return only the finished English artifact unless explanation was requested.
