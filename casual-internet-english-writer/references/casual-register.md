# Casual internet register

Casual internet English is not professional prose with slang added. Match the user, thread, relationship, and artifact before using general forum patterns.

## Working register

- Start with the actual contribution. Remove empty `Great point` openers, recaps, or polite preambles; keep brief agreement or courtesy when it is part of the user's message.
- Use direct personal agency and ordinary verbs where they fit. Keep precise technical language in technical discussions.
- Contractions, uneven sentence lengths, brief fragments, sentence-initial conjunctions, compact asides, and abrupt endings can all be natural. Use them for a real function, not by quota.
- Keep uncertainty next to its claim. Do not turn mixed feelings into a ritual both-sides essay or remove a real caveat to sound decisive.
- Preserve harmless irregularity already supported by the user's voice. Repair ambiguity and distracting damage, not every departure from formal grammar.
- End when the point is made. Do not append a lesson, summary, or engagement question by default.

## Review cues

Inspect:

- `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `Additionally`, `It is important to note`, or `In conclusion` in a simple post or reply;
- generic agreement followed by a restatement;
- perfectly staged contrasts, repeated parallel slogans, or empty balanced hedging;
- disclaimer and hedge stacks;
- bureaucratic subjects, noun piles, and abstract framing where the speaker could state the action;
- every sentence and paragraph having the same polished shape;
- nested asides that bury the point.

Remove only what creates the mismatch. Preserve genuine logic, comparisons, uncertainty, citations, definitions, code, and a short voice-bearing aside. Em dashes, semicolons, fragments, and complete grammar are not automatic evidence of anything; repeated or conspicuously polished use is the issue.

## No internet costume

Do not introduce `tbh`, `ngl`, `lol`, meme phrases, profanity, lowercase `i`, missing apostrophes, ellipsis chains, or typos merely because the artifact is casual. Existing samples may support some of these, but never copy a stranger's quirks or manufacture errors. Natural variation comes from the thought, not sabotage.

Read the result as a participant. If it sounds like a miniature essay, remove scaffolding. If it sounds like someone performing Reddit, remove the costume.

## Conditional repairs

Apply these to editable prose, not quotations, code, cited titles, or exact technical labels. A word is a review cue, not an authorship test. Prefer the smallest repair that preserves the contribution.

| When the draft does this | Repair | Keep when it does real work |
|---|---|---|
| Opens with `Great question!`, `Certainly!`, an era frame, or a recap before answering | Start at the answer, observation, or supplied scene | A brief `Yeah` or `Absolutely` expresses the user's actual agreement; it need not be stripped |
| Uses `delve into`, `navigate the complexities`, or a figurative `rich tapestry` without a concrete referent | Name the actual action or subject: `look at`, `work through`, or the supplied description | Literal subjects, deliberate imagery, and the user's established voice |
| Uses `utilize`, `facilitate`, `commence`, or `stands as` to inflate an ordinary action | Try `use`, `help`, `start`, or `is`, checking the meaning | Domain language: `CPU utilization`, `terminate a process`, `dynamic typing` are not filler |
| Calls something `robust`, `seamless`, or a `game-changer` without saying what changed | Use a supplied benefit, constraint, or behavior; otherwise remove the empty intensifier | Genuine enthusiasm or an actual technical property |
| Manufactures `It's not just X, it's Y` or a repeated three-part slogan | State the contribution directly; remove only redundant framing | A real correction, contrast, or three distinct facts. Never drop X merely to remove the pattern |
| Ends with `highlighting the importance of...` or `underscoring our commitment...` | End at the fact, or state an already-supported consequence | A clause carrying actual action, cause, or scope, such as `using the same cable` |
| Poses `Why does this matter? Because...` only to dramatize a simple claim | Integrate the answer into the claim | The user's actual question or an explanation whose question genuinely orients the reader |
| Uses a transition at every turn, nested asides, or an identical paragraph template | Keep the needed link, move a buried qualification beside its claim, and merge or split where the thought changes | `But`, `so`, `because`, a longer technical sentence, or a compact aside that clarifies the point |
| Ends each paragraph with its own takeaway | Stop at the last contribution | A supplied punchline, emotional ending, or requested summary |

## Grounding and rhythm pass

Ask what ties this message to this conversation. Use the supplied constraint, error, question, or event; `here` and `yesterday` are useful only when the source establishes them. A one-line reaction does not need an invented number to justify its existence.

If a sentence could fit almost any thread, check its function before cutting it: a generic claim may be filler, but `Thanks, that worked` has a clear conversational purpose. Replace vague filler using available facts, or simplify it. Ask for a missing detail only if the message cannot be completed faithfully without it.

Read consecutive sentences for repeated jobs and repeated shapes. Split a crowded explanation at its actual turn; join theatrical fragments that have no independent emphasis. Do not alternate short and long sentences mechanically. A brief answer can stay brief, and a careful technical explanation can stay long.

Preserve independent qualifiers. `I think it only happens on older phones` contains uncertainty and a scope limit, not two redundant hedges. Remove repeated uncertainty, not the limitation.
