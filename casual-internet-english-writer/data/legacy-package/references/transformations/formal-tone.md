# Formal tone without useful formality

Read when bureaucratic openings, nominalizations, inflated verbs, or impersonal descriptions of personal actions obscure a casual message. Formal vocabulary is a review cue, not a prohibited-word list.

## Decision and repair

1. Identify the actual actor, action, recipient, and force of the sentence.
2. Replace an inflated expression only when the simpler form carries the same meaning. `use` often replaces `utilize`; `press` only fits an actual button/key action. Do not turn every term into a phrasal verb.
3. Remove a ceremonial opening if it adds no politeness or context needed by this recipient.
4. Prefer personal subjects only when the speaker is actually that person. Do not convert reported experience into the user's first-person testimony.
5. Keep literal technical meanings, proper names, emphasis, and the user's supported level of courtesy. No automatic slang or profanity.

## Counterexample and check

Do not rewrite "You must submit it Friday" as "Could you send it Friday?" unless the user asks to soften an obligation into a request. Do not add a price, personal experience, or promised outcome to make the prose concrete. Check actor, modality, and recipient after simplification.
