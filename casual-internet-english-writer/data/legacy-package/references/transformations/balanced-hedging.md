# Balanced Hedging

Read when paired perspectives, mirrored caveats, or stacked uncertainty phrases obscure the actual answer. Review cues include `on the one hand ... on the other hand`, `some argue ... others contend`, and `valid points on both sides`. They can also express a legitimate comparison.

## Detect and repair

1. Establish whether the user needs an opinion, a fair comparison, an evidence-based conclusion, or a personal boundary.
2. Separate informational uncertainty from politeness, real competing priorities, and empty symmetry. Keep the first three where they matter.
3. Remove a duplicate hedge only when one qualifier preserves its complete scope. `might` is not interchangeable with `will`, and a personal judgment is not a universal fact.
4. If evidence supports a conclusion, state it with the relevant reason or condition. If it does not, state what is unknown or which preference decides. Never force a side for stylistic confidence.
5. Keep both sides when the user requests a comparison or the trade-off is material. A clear contrast is not evasive by definition.

## Faithful transformation matrix

Constructed fixtures; their facts are supplied within the examples only.

| Input | Meaning-preserving output | Invariant |
|---|---|---|
| "I think it might possibly arrive Friday." | "I think it might arrive Friday." | Uncertain estimate, not a promise |
| "On the one hand, plan A is cheaper. On the other hand, plan B includes backups." | "Plan A is cheaper. Plan B includes backups." | Both relevant options, no invented verdict |
| "It is a complex issue with valid points on both sides, but I can't take this on this week." | "I can't take this on this week." | Speaker's boundary; no substantive trade-off was given |
| "It may fail on older phones, though we haven't tested them." | Unchanged if this is the available evidence. | Both risk and testing gap |
| "Reviews are mixed, and I haven't tried it." | Unchanged without further evidence. | Mixed reports and lack of first-hand knowledge |

## Counterexample and check

Do not convert "Some trials found no benefit; others found a small benefit" into "It works" or "It doesn't work." Do not remove an attribution to make a claim sound personal. Two feelings can coexist without rhetorical evasion. Check the speaker's stance, the evidence strength, and every remaining condition after editing.
