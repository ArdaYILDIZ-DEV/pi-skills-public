# Convoluted phrasing

Read when noun stacks, repeated prepositional phrases, or embedded clauses make the actor and action hard to recover. Three adjacent nouns are an inspection cue, not a ban on precise compound terms.

## Decision and repair

1. Identify who did what, to what, and under which condition. Preserve any unknown actor rather than inventing one.
2. Turn a nominalization into a verb when that reveals the same action.
3. Unpack competing claims into sentences without separating a qualification from its claim.
4. Retain domain terms, units, thresholds, and causal uncertainty. Everyday words can be less precise, not more helpful.
5. Use an analogy only when the audience needs one and its mapping is defensible. It supplements an accurate explanation; it does not replace evidence.

## Faithful transformation matrix

Constructed fixtures, not empirical language samples.

| Input | Meaning-preserving output | Invariant |
|---|---|---|
| "The replacement of the cable resulted in the restoration of the connection." | "Replacing the cable restored the connection." | Supplied causal claim, no invented actor |
| "I experienced difficulty with the completion of the upload." | "I had trouble finishing the upload." | Difficulty, not proof of total failure |
| "Connection restoration may require router replacement." | "You may need to replace the router to restore the connection." | Possible requirement, not a guaranteed fix |
| "The database uses multi-version concurrency control." | Unchanged if this is the intended technical term. | Exact concept |

## Counterexample and check

"The change was associated with fewer errors" cannot become "The change fixed the errors." Association is not cause, and fewer is not zero. An engaging household analogy is not a replacement for these distinctions. Check subject, action, dependency, causality, and degree after untangling.
