# Voice and register

Read for every in-scope writing task. Choose the speaker's voice; do not impose a generic casual persona. The [entry point](../SKILL.md) defines meaning and permission guards.

## Calibrate from the actual user

Use this order:

1. Explicit task constraints: meaning, audience, purpose, language, length, and requested tone.
2. The current source and comparable user samples, including Turkish. Turkish frustration, restraint, humor, informality, and firmness are usable voice evidence even without an English sample.
3. Supplied thread context and relationship. Do not claim knowledge of subreddit rules that were not provided or actually checked under authorization.
4. Bundled illustrations only when helpful. They are neither user history nor permission to imitate strangers.

Carry over communicative force, not every surface feature: no automatic slang for Turkish particles, apologies for direct complaints, or rants for restraint. Plain English is not compulsory politeness.

Default to conversational, unshowy English; adjust courtesy to the recipient. Do not add `tbh`, `ngl`, `lol`, pet names, or rapport merely because the source is casual. Preserve supplied profanity's force without escalation; an explicit request to remove it wins.

## When to ask

Do not make routine translation or a well-specified post/comment depend on voice samples. Ask one focused question when a missing fact, recipient relationship, or intended stance would produce materially different messages. Ask about the missing stance itself; samples cannot tell you whether the user agrees. In explicitly delicate outreach with no usable source voice and unresolved tone, ask for two short samples. Follow the entry point's response-language rule for questions, without a translated duplicate. Never invent a sensitive employment, family, or relationship history to avoid asking.

## Artifact decisions

| Artifact | Default | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Translation of personal prose | Preserve scope and tone; use idiomatic English | Summary, extra title, advice, or rewriting the opinion |
| Reddit comment/reply | Address the actual point, then necessary support | Generic agreement opener, title, lecture, invented experience |
| Reddit post | A grounded title and readable body unless otherwise requested | Clickbait, forced debate, fake posting history |
| Routine email | A direct, appropriately polite message | Ceremonial opening, invented urgency, unnecessary intake question |
| Friend DM | Match relationship and directness | Invented intimacy, apology, pressure, or reassurance |
| Technical discussion | Preserve identifiers and distinctions | Replacing precise terminology with vague everyday words |
| Personal story | Supplied chronology and emotional weight | Added dialogue, diagnosis, lesson, or climax |
| Support/grief message | Address the recipient's actual need | Advice stacking, fabricated shared experience, staged distress |

Keep requested summaries and useful lists, including readable procedures. Neither length nor conversational tone permits dropping detail.

## Surface choices

- Use contractions where natural; keep deliberate emphasis such as “I do not agree.”
- Standard capitalization is the default. Current samples or explicit styling requests may establish lowercase; do not borrow it from archived samples.
- Do not manufacture dialect, errors, apostrophe loss, or slang. Preserve supported informal phrasing without reproducing accidental damage mechanically.
- No emoji or em dashes in authored drafts. Keep exact quoted evidence intact when verbatim preservation is required.
- Do not replace em dashes with comma splices. Join independent clauses with a conjunction or separate them.
- No fabricated `Edit:`, `ETA:`, live updates, or promises to return. A real supplied update can be formatted as one.

## Constructed contrasts

| Source intent | Faithful | Drift |
|---|---|---|
| Firm refusal this week | “I can't take this on this week.” | “I'll try to make time.” |
| Uncertain arrival | “It might arrive Tuesday.” | “It'll arrive Tuesday.” |
| Annoyed but restrained | “Having to set it up again is annoying.” | “This is completely unacceptable.” |
| Polite request to a supplier | “Could you send the updated quote?” | “yo send the quote lol” |
| No longer willing to spend effort | “I don't want to keep dealing with this.” | “I don't care what happens.” |

Check the resulting speaker, not just the vocabulary: did you strengthen a promise, weaken a boundary, add an experience, or change how they feel? Repair that before polishing rhythm.
