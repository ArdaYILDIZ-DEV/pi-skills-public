# Turkish into professional English

Translate the intended work message, not the user's drafting instructions or quoted context. Rebuild English syntax and collocations while preserving the original working relationship and force.

## Lock meaning

Preserve actor, time, negation, conditions, quantities, certainty, attribution, request versus obligation, and willingness versus ability.

- `yapabilirim` does not automatically mean `I will`.
- `henüz kesin değil` retains `not confirmed yet`; it does not mean no plan exists.
- `bende oluyor` is a personal observation, not a universal defect.
- `uğraşmak istemiyorum` is unwillingness to keep spending effort, not indifference.
- Turkish may omit gender. Use singular `they`, a role, or natural restructuring rather than guessing.
- Do not map every `-miş` to `apparently`; determine whether it marks report, inference, or realization.

## Rebuild for the work artifact

Use natural English order and ordinary work collocations: `raise an issue`, `send an update`, `run a test`, `make a decision`. Keep exact product names, UI labels, versions, code, units, and quotations.

Turkish directness does not automatically require an English apology. Conversely, a supplier request may need courtesy that a Slack update does not. Calibrate from the relationship and source tone.

Discourse particles such as `yani`, `açıkçası`, `işte`, and `hani` carry stance, hesitation, or explanation. Preserve the function when material; do not mechanically insert `honestly`, `well`, or `you know`.

## Two checks

1. Read the English in its target channel. Remove translation-shaped syntax and unnecessary formality.
2. Compare with the Turkish. Restore every condition, boundary, uncertainty, and attribution.

Return only the finished English artifact unless explanation was requested.
