# Professional register

Natural professional English is competent communication between people doing work. Match the artifact, relationship, and consequence rather than applying one polished corporate voice.

## Working register

- Put the request, observation, decision, or blocker early. Keep courtesy when it serves the relationship; remove ceremonial framing that delays a routine point.
- Prefer concrete actors and verbs when responsibility matters: `I checked`, `we need`, `the job failed`. Keep an impersonal form when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Use contractions naturally in conversational work artifacts. Uncontracted forms remain appropriate in specifications, policies, formal audiences, or deliberate emphasis.
- Keep domain terms exact. Plain English removes bureaucracy, not precision.
- Put uncertainty next to its claim: `I think this is coming from the cache`, `This may fail on older clients`, `We haven't tested that path yet`.
- Ground disagreement in the relevant reason, evidence, or risk. Do not manufacture praise before every correction.
- Brief fragments and shorthand can fit chat, triage, and review. Do not inject theatrical pacing.
- Stop when the recipient has what they need. A useful thanks or sign-off is fine; a summary that repeats the message is not.

## Review cues

Inspect rather than automatically ban:

- ceremonial openings and canned closers;
- repeated `Furthermore`, `Moreover`, `Additionally`, or similar signposting;
- nominalizations or passives that hide a known actor;
- `On the one hand... on the other hand...` when the writer already has a position or the recipient needs a decision;
- stacked hedges or disclaimer paragraphs;
- nested parentheses that bury the action;
- mechanically parallel sentences and paragraphs.

Repair only the mismatch. Use a shared subject, direct verb, nearby qualifier, concrete option, or plain sentence boundary. Preserve genuine comparisons, required citations, first-use acronym definitions, code, dates, thresholds, and compact scope notes.

Ask whether a competent colleague would send this in this channel. If it sounds like a press release, academic abstract, customer-service script, or assistant explanation, revise the lines causing that mismatch. Do not make it slangier to compensate.
