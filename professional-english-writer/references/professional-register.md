# Professional register

Natural professional English is competent communication between people doing work. Match the artifact, relationship, and consequence rather than applying one polished corporate voice.

## Working register

- Put the request, observation, decision, or blocker early. Keep courtesy when it serves the relationship; remove ceremonial framing that delays a routine point.
- Prefer concrete actors and verbs when responsibility matters: `I checked`, `we need`, `the job failed`. Keep an impersonal form when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Use contractions naturally in conversational work artifacts. Uncontracted forms remain appropriate in specifications, policies, formal audiences, or deliberate emphasis.
- Keep domain terms exact. Plain English removes bureaucracy, not precision.
- Put uncertainty next to its claim: `I think this is coming from the cache`, `This may fail on older clients`, `We haven't tested that path yet`.
- Ground disagreement in the relevant reason, evidence, or risk. Do not manufacture praise before every correction.
- Brief fragments and shorthand can fit chat, triage, and review. Do not inject theatrical pacing.
- Stop when the recipient has what they need. A useful thanks or sign-off is fine; a summary that repeats the message is not.

## Review cues

Inspect rather than automatically ban:

- ceremonial openings and canned closers;
- repeated `Furthermore`, `Moreover`, `Additionally`, or similar signposting;
- nominalizations or passives that hide a known actor;
- `On the one hand... on the other hand...` when the writer already has a position or the recipient needs a decision;
- stacked hedges or disclaimer paragraphs;
- nested parentheses that bury the action;
- mechanically parallel sentences and paragraphs.

Repair only the mismatch. Use a shared subject, direct verb, nearby qualifier, concrete option, or plain sentence boundary. Preserve genuine comparisons, required citations, first-use acronym definitions, code, dates, thresholds, and compact scope notes.

Ask whether a competent colleague would send this in this channel. If it sounds like a press release, academic abstract, customer-service script, or assistant explanation, revise the lines causing that mismatch. Do not make it slangier to compensate.

## Conditional repairs

Use plain language around exact domain terms. Do not run a synonym replacement across code, quotations, names, legal wording, or technical concepts. Sentence patterns are editorial cues, not authorship evidence.

| When the draft does this | Repair | Preserve this distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Delays a routine request with `I hope this email finds you well` or an announcement of what follows | Lead with the reason or request; retain relational courtesy where useful | An external email is not necessarily a Slack message; apologies, thanks, and sign-offs can be substantive |
| Uses `utilize`, `commence`, `ascertain`, or `disseminate` for an ordinary action | Consider `use`, `start`, `find out`, or `send/share` | No etymology rule: `CPU utilization`, `terminate the connection`, and `demonstrate a failure` may be exact |
| Hides a known actor in `the implementation of the optimization` | State who changed what, if the source identifies them | Do not guess an owner; `The deploy is paused` is fine when state matters more than agency |
| Uses `serves as`, `boasts`, or `stands as` where `is` or `has` carries the same meaning | Restore the simple verb | `Acts as a proxy` can express a role; `represents` can express a mapping rather than identity |
| Labels work `robust`, `seamless`, `transformative`, or `comprehensive` instead of describing it | Use the supplied behavior, coverage, limit, or measurement; otherwise state the plain change | Never invent a benchmark, guarantee, compatibility claim, or improvement mechanism |
| Uses a manufactured `not just X, but Y` contrast or adjective triplet | Remove ornamental framing and preserve each substantive proposition | Corrections, alternatives, inventories, and actual three-part requirements remain intact |
| Adds `highlighting our commitment` or `ensuring seamless integration` after a factual update | Delete empty significance, or state a supported consequence separately | Purpose, expected effect, and measured result are different: `intended to reduce` must not become `reduced` |
| Stacks `could potentially perhaps` or ritual both-sides framing | Keep the actual uncertainty and relevant options; remove redundant qualification | `I think this may fail on older clients` may carry both diagnostic uncertainty and conditional risk |
| Repeats `Moreover`, bold lead-ins, rhetorical questions, or summary closers | Use the structure needed to navigate the artifact, without restating every point | Long documents may need headings, transitions, an executive summary, or a requested recommendation |

## Information and cadence pass

For each abstract sentence, ask what it lets the recipient understand or do. Replace portable promotional language with available facts, not invented specifics. A short operational statement such as `No change needed` can be useful without a metric or proper noun.

Check that adjacent sentences do different jobs. Remove repeated status, combine a shared subject where clearer, or separate action from a buried limitation. Do not enforce numerical rhythm bands, an em-dash allowance, or uneven bullet lengths. Parallel structure helps people compare real options and steps.

Keep scope and confidence attached to the correct claim. Removing `only`, `so far`, `in staging`, `if`, or `not yet` can change a technical statement more than any stylistic edit improves it. Do not remove these to satisfy a hedge limit.
