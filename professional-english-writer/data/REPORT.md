# Natural practitioner professional English

Across the examined GitHub pull requests, code reviews, issue discussions, mailing-list threads, and practitioner essays, writers tend to front the concrete problem or proposal, use active agency, qualify uncertainty next to the relevant claim, and connect ideas without ceremonial scaffolding. [S1–S11]

The useful distinction is not formal versus casual vocabulary. Natural professional writing remains precise, but it avoids bureaucratic padding, ritual both-sides framing, and canned conclusions when those forms do no work.

Generation guidance should be artifact-specific: a PR or Slack-style update can be terse and fragmentary; an email may need courtesy; a technical essay may use a longer framing question; specifications and contracts may legitimately remain formal and uncontracted.

The inherited natural-english-writer package strengthens the fidelity and anti-polish workflow, but its historical corpus counts and numerical rhythm claims are mixed-evidence inputs rather than verified statistics. [S12]

Unknowns: the public sample overrepresents open-source engineering. Private enterprise email, executive communication, regulated writing, and nontechnical industries may require different courtesy and structure.
